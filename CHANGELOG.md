## [Unreleased]

- **The window is built on the docking frame of the suite (`eh3d-dock`):** the tabs are panels that can be taken away, put back, moved, floated over the window, folded into an edge, maximised and locked; the window starts in one of three arrangements (Setup, Run and Compact) and has a menu bar with File (samples and recent files), Edit, View, Tools, Window and Help and the usual shortcuts; what the person arranged is kept in `window.cfg` under `dock.` keys, and a layout that is damaged or from another version gives the factory one with a notice. The camera calibrations moved to a panel of their own.
- **A panel for each of eleven commands:** `driver-check`, `driver-test`, `driver-run`, `safety-replay`, `homing-repeat`, `bringup`, `import-openpnp`, `assist`, `align-iterate`, `vacuum-calibrate` and `test-board-gen` can be run from a panel, which runs `phoenix-pnp` beside the window with the arguments typed in it (bounded, no shell, no console window, stopped after `command_timeout_s` seconds, output cut at a limit) and shows what it printed.
- **Build:** the sibling EH3D-COMMON is taken at v0.2.7, the version that carries the docking frame; `Cargo.toml` depends on its `eh3d-dock` crate (the drawing needs the feature `gui`).
- **Interlocks of the axes:** a line `interlock = SIGNAL | on|off | xy|z|rot|all` of a driver profile keeps the axes it names from moving while the signal is not in the state it names; the signals are the actuators of the profile and the names that a sensor is given (`Session::set_signal`), and a signal that nobody has reported is unknown and refuses the move. A refused move stops the session after the lines of `safe_stop`, as a move outside the limits does. Both example profiles keep the carriage still in X and Y while the blow valve is on.
- **A process for the guided manual mode:** `phoenix-pnp assist PARTS --process pnp|manual|FILE` walks a board phase by phase (load, place a side, oven, solder by hand, look): the parts of a side placed by hand one at a time, the other tasks until the person says `done`, and a side that the machine places is told. A phase starts only after the earlier ones, and its load-out cannot change once it has begun; the state can be saved and taken back in.
- **Requirements with identifiers and tests that name them:** `docs/REQUIREMENTS.md` is the register of what the program must do, one row for each capability with an identifier (`PPNP-001` on), its state (planned, prototype, implemented or validated), how far it is (done, partial, pending or blocked), its evidence and the test that shows it; a capability that is not written names the test that will show it. `tests/reqs.rs` reads the table and fails when a test does not exist, when a state does not agree with the progress or the evidence, when `docs/STATUS.md` or a README does not say the same numbers, or when a document holds a date or a private reference. The five documents of the family (`docs/SCOPE.md`, `docs/REQUIREMENTS.md`, `docs/STATUS.md`, `docs/NOT_YET.md` and `AGENTS.md`) are in the repository and are linked from the eight READMEs.

## [0.1.3] - Files for assembly houses, a declarative G-code driver for Marlin and Klipper with a simulated controller, an importer of OpenPnP files, a guided manual assistant and checks that only move toward the safer side

- **A pattern matcher with a bounded time (`textmatch`):** the lines a controller answers are read with patterns from a file (`^ok`, `X:(-?\d+\.?\d*) Y:(-?\d+\.?\d*)`). The matcher knows letters, `.`, `\d \D \s \S \w \W`, classes, `* + ?`, groups that capture and groups that do not, `|`, `^`, `$` and a `(?i)` at the start; it runs every thread of the pattern one letter at a time, so the time is the length of the text times the size of the pattern whatever the pattern is (`(a*)*b` on four thousand letters comes back at once), a pattern over 256 letters, with more than 9 groups, nested over 16 deep or over 2000 steps is refused with what is wrong, and a text over 4096 letters is cut. Tests cover the syntax, the refusals, the bounded time and patterns and texts made by chance on a fixed seed that never panic.
- **The profile of a G-code driver is a file (`driver-check`):** `phoenix-pnp driver-check PROFILE` reads a profile of `key = value` lines (`examples/driver_marlin.cfg` for Marlin on a serial line, `examples/driver_klipper.cfg` for Klipper through Moonraker) with the lines that connect, home and leave the machine safe, the mode of the coordinates, the letters of the axes (the one that turns the nozzle may be the `E` of the firmware, or none), the lines that wait and ask for the position, the template of a dwell, the pins that switch (`actuator = name | on | off`, the pump and the release), the patterns of an answer, the feeds, heights and limits, the times, the retries and the rules of safety. Every key has its range; an unknown key, a key given twice, a pattern that does not compile, an actuator that nothing defines and values that contradict each other (a feed over the greatest, a lift under a pick, a slower step faster than the one before) are errors with their line, in the eight languages. The profile has been tried only against the simulated controller.
- **The lines of a driver (`gcodedriver`):** a move is the command, the axes that are given and the feed, and is always followed by the line that waits for it; a place outside the limits, a feed over the greatest or a number that is not one is refused and never cut down; a placement is written as up, over the feeder, down, pump on, up, over the place with the turn, down, pump off, blow, up, and one for a nozzle the profile does not have is refused before any line is sent.
- **Answers read by the profile (`driver-test`):** `phoenix-pnp driver-test PROFILE TRANSCRIPT` plays recorded answers (`expected | line`, `examples/marlin_answers.txt` and `examples/klipper_answers.txt`: an `ok` with data after it, a position with the `E` axis and without, the `Count` block, `echo:busy`, `echo:Unknown command`, `Error:Printer halted`) against the patterns of a profile and names, with its line, each row that is read as something else; a pattern that takes `echo:SD card ok` for an answer, or loses the turn of a position, is found before a machine is. The cases are written by hand and none is a capture of a real machine.
- **Requests of Moonraker:** a script is a `POST` with the lines joined in `{"script": ...}`, the position is a `GET` of the toolhead, an answer under 300 is ok and any other gives the message of its error (or `HTTP n`), and the position of the toolhead is read from `result.status.toolhead.position`; a profile for Klipper must have patterns that read what the link writes (`ok`, `!! message`, a position line) or it is refused.
- **A simulated Marlin and Klipper controller (`gcodesim`):** it answers `G28`, `G0`/`G1` (refused before the homing and out of range), `G4`, `M400` (after the time the moves need), `M114` with the `E` axis and the `Count` block, `M115`, `M42`, `M106`, `M84` and the `SET_PIN` of Klipper as the dialect does, keeps a clock, and can be given faults on purpose: an error at the Nth line, a line that gets no answer, a halted controller, `busy` lines, a position that is not the real one, a homing that lands a little off, slow moves. The same seed gives the same run.
- **`driver-run`:** `phoenix-pnp driver-run PARTS FEEDERS PROFILE [--fault ...] [--seed S] [--script-out F] [--log-out F]` plans the board, writes the plan as the lines of the profile (`--script-out`) and sends it to a simulated controller of the dialect of the profile with its timeouts and retries (`--log-out` keeps every line with its result). A query or a switch that gets no answer is asked again; a move is not sent twice: when the wait for it times out the session asks where the head is and goes on only if it is at the target; an error of the controller, a retry that is not enough, a position that is not the one asked for or a refused move stop it after the lines of `safe_stop`, and nothing more is sent. It prints `evidence=simulator` and opens no port.
- **Rules that only ever make the machine safer (`safety-replay`):** after a retry, a timeout or a refused move the feed is cut to `safety_careful_speed_pct`, then to `safety_slow_speed_pct`, then the machine is held (`safety_careful_events`, `safety_slow_events`, `safety_hold_events`); a position that is not the one asked for or a lost vacuum hold it at once and an error of the controller stops it. A good answer gives nothing back, no event lowers a level, and only a person (`acknowledge`) brings a held machine back, to slow and never to normal; a stopped one stays stopped. Figures that would let a trouble speed the machine up are refused. `phoenix-pnp safety-replay EVENTS` replays a file of events (`examples/safety_events.txt`) and exits with 1 when the machine ends held or stopped. The tests try every sequence of up to five events and 500 long runs made by chance, and fail if a change relaxes a limit.
- **`homing-repeat`:** `phoenix-pnp homing-repeat PROFILE [--runs N] [--jitter MM] [--max-spread MM] [--seed S]` homes a simulated machine N times and says how far apart (in X and in Y) the places it lands at are, against `homing_spread_mm` of the profile; the exit code is 1 over the limit. It tests the arithmetic and the profile, not a real homing.
- **`bringup`:** `phoenix-pnp bringup PROFILE [--fault ...]` does the steps of a first start on a simulated controller (connect, home, ask the position, switch each actuator on and off, move round a square of `bringup_probe_mm`, close) and prints PASS or FAIL for each; the exit code is 1 on a FAIL.
- **Look, move, look again (`align-iterate`):** `phoenix-pnp align-iterate [--error X,Y] [--noise MM] [--scale K] [--seed S]` brings the head to a mark in small steps against a camera that is made up: it looks, moves `lml_gain` of what it saw, and looks again, until what is left is under `lml_tolerance_mm`, with no step over `lml_max_step_mm` and at most `lml_max_iterations` moves. A look that gets worse halves the cap and the gain for the rest of the run (they never go up again), two worse looks in a row stop it, and a look that cannot be read stops it with no move. `lml_offset_x_mm` and `lml_offset_y_mm` take off the constant error of the camera against the nozzle. The exit code is 1 when the head does not align. It is arithmetic over looks that are made up: it moves nothing.
- **A bounded reader of XML (`xmlmini`):** the shape of a document (elements, attributes, what is inside) with limits of 8 MiB, 32 levels, 200000 elements, 64 attributes, 128 letters in a name and 4096 in a value; a document type or an entity is refused so that nothing is expanded, only the five entities of XML and the numeric ones are read, and the error says the line, in the eight languages. Cut at every place and damaged by chance on a fixed seed, it never panics.
- **`import-openpnp` reads the files of another program and never writes to them:** `phoenix-pnp import-openpnp [--machine F] [--packages F] [--parts F] [--out-dir D]` reads the packages (the body of the footprint), the parts (the package and the height) and the feeders of a machine (the part, the place it is picked from, the count) into a table of packages, a table of parts and a table of feeders (`packages.csv`, `catalog.csv` and `feeders.csv` in `--out-dir`, never over a file that exists) with the units of the file (millimetres, centimetres, metres, inches, feet, mils, microns) brought to millimetres; a feeder takes its package from its part. Every field it cannot map is told with its file and line: an unknown unit, a value that is not a number, a missing field, a name that is there twice, a part of a package that is not in the packages, a feeder that is switched off, and the heads, cameras and actuators that are counted and not mapped. A row it cannot use is an error and is left out, and the exit code is 1. The example files (`examples/openpnp_*.xml`) are written for the tests and are not the files of any machine; the program has not been tried with real ones.
- **A guided manual mode (`assist`):** `phoenix-pnp assist PARTS [--feeders F] [--order reel|reference] [--answers F] [--resume F] [--out F]` walks a person through the board with no machine: the parts of one reel together (or by reference), the top side before the bottom, each in the natural order of its reference, with the reel it comes from and its place, its turn, its side and whether it has a direction (`polarity_refs`, `polarity_packages`). The person types the reference of the part just placed and the walk goes on only if it is the right one; `skip`, `back` and `quit` do what they say, skipped parts come back at the end, and the checklist (`--out`) can be read again with `--resume`. The answers come from the keyboard or from a file (`examples/assist_answers.txt`), in the eight languages.
- **`vacuum-calibrate`:** `phoenix-pnp vacuum-calibrate READINGS [--out F]` reads a table `state,kpa` (`free`, `held`, `released`; `examples/vacuum_readings.csv`) and proposes `vacuum_pick_min_kpa` and `vacuum_release_max_kpa` a margin (`vacuum_margin_pct`) inside the gap between the strongest reading with no part and the weakest with one, as two lines that can be pasted in the settings. It tells when there are few readings (`vacuum_min_readings`), when the gap is small (`vacuum_min_gap_kpa`), how many readings the thresholds in use now would misread and, when the two kinds overlap, that no threshold can tell them apart (exit code 1).
- **`test-board-gen`:** `phoenix-pnp test-board-gen grid|mixed|dense PARTS_OUT FEEDERS_OUT [--seed S] [--count N] [--pitch MM]` writes a board made up from a seed and the feeders that hold it: 0402 resistors on a grid, passives, LEDs and chips on both sides turned in the four directions on places that do not repeat, or a dense grid; the same seed gives the same bytes, so two machines or two settings can be compared on the same board (`testboard_count`, `testboard_pitch_mm`).
- **Numbers that are text:** `tests/textvalues.rs` feeds `0402`, `007`, `1e3`, `0.10`, `00100` and `+5` as a reference, a value, a package, a feeder, a part number and a lot to every reader and writer of a table and fails if one of them is turned into a number.
- **New keys of the settings:** `lml_max_step_mm`, `lml_tolerance_mm`, `lml_max_iterations`, `lml_gain`, `lml_offset_x_mm`, `lml_offset_y_mm`, `vacuum_margin_pct`, `vacuum_min_gap_kpa`, `vacuum_min_readings`, `testboard_pitch_mm` and `testboard_count`, each with its range, its line explained in `examples/machine.cfg` and a default that changes nothing that was there; the figures of the driver (feeds, heights, limits, times, retries, rules of safety) are in the profile file and not in the code.
- **Tests of the driver and of the import:** `tests/textmatch.rs`, `tests/driver.rs`, `tests/safety.rs`, `tests/drivercli.rs`, `tests/lookmove.rs`, `tests/vacuumcal.rs`, `tests/openpnp.rs`, `tests/assist.rs`, `tests/importcli.rs` and `tests/textvalues.rs` check the profile and its errors in the eight languages, the lines it writes, the answers it reads, a session with every fault, the rules of safety over every short sequence of events and long ones by chance, the files read by the import cut at every place and damaged on a fixed seed, and every new command as a person runs it.
- **Known limits told:** the README says that the driver has been tried only against a simulated controller (nothing opens a port and no machine has been moved), that the answers of the examples are written by hand and not captured, that the import has not been tried with real files of the other program, and that the guided mode and the made-up camera are not a machine.
- **The pictures of the window are taken again with the newest build:** every picture of `images/screens/` comes from the Windows 64 bits program of the latest release, in the eight languages, and the README files show a new picture of the Machine tab with the server section.
- **`export-fab`:** `phoenix-pnp export-fab PARTS [--bom BOM] [--side all|top|bottom] [--mount] [--dnp-mode column|omit] [--cpl-out F] [--bom-out F]` writes the placement list that board assembly houses ask for (`Designator`, `Mid X`, `Mid Y`, `Layer`, `Rotation`): one part on each line, the places in two columns with their unit written, the layer `Top` or `Bottom`, the rotation from 0 up to 360, the parts in the natural order of their designators. With no output named the list is printed; the files are never written over a file that exists (`jlcpcb::cpl`).
- **The parts that are not to be mounted, in the files for a house:** a part that a table marks as not to be mounted stays in the placement list and in the bill and is marked in a column whose name is the setting `fab_dnp_column` (`Customer Supply` by default), or is left out of both with `fab_dnp_mode = omit` or `--dnp-mode omit`.
- **Parts that go through the board:** the list holds the parts soldered on the surface and the ones that go through the board, one line each; `--mount` adds the column `Mount` (`SMD` or `THT`). A part goes through the board when its package holds one of the words of the setting `fab_tht_words` (`tht`, `dip`, `axial`, `radial`, `pinheader`, ... as whole words, so `Dipole_0603` does not).
- **The bill of materials for a house:** `--bom-out F` writes `MPN`, `Designator`, `Quantity`, `Description` and `Footprint` with a row for each value, package and part number, the part numbers taken by designator from the bill of the CAD program given with `--bom`, and the designators of a row in one cell in the natural order (`jlcpcb::bom_rows`, `jlcpcb::bom_csv`).
- **`fab-check`:** `phoenix-pnp fab-check FILE` reads a placement list and names, with its line, what an assembly house would refuse: a header without the designator, the places or the layer, an empty designator, a list or a range in the cell of a designator, a designator twice, a place that is not a number, a layer that is not `Top` or `Bottom` and a rotation that is not a number. `export-fab` runs the same check on what it writes (`jlcpcb::check_cpl`).
- **Designators in the natural order and as ranges:** `designators::natural_cmp` puts `R2` before `R10` and compares numbers of any size without overflowing, `designators::compress` writes runs of three or more as a range (`R1-3`) that `import::expand_references` reads back, whatever the order of the list, and `designators::is_single` tells one designator from a list or a range.
- **`convert-cpl`:** `phoenix-pnp convert-cpl FILE [--from kicad|altium|eagle|jlcpcb|generic] [--to jlcpcb|cpl|kicad-pos|generic] [--origin X,Y] [--flip-y] [--cw] [--rot-offset DEG] [--bottom keep|mirror|half_turn] [--out F]` tells the dialect of a position file from its header or its first row (KiCad `.pos` and CSV, Altium with units in the header, EAGLE with `R90` and `MR90`, the list of a house with `Mid X`), reads it in millimetres and degrees with the parts that are not to be mounted kept apart (found by the column of mounting of a table or by the column `fab_dnp_column` of a list of a house), and writes it in the shape asked for (`cplconv::detect`, `cplconv::read`).
- **Origin and rotation of a conversion:** `cplconv::convert` moves the origin, flips the Y axis (a mirror also reverses the rotation, and two reversals cancel), counts the rotation the other way for a file that counts clockwise, adds a turn and applies the rule of the bottom side, always in that order; a number that is not finite or a place farther than a million millimetres is refused with the code of a bad table of parts. `cplconv::undo` gives the parts back. The defaults are the settings `cpl_origin_x_mm`, `cpl_origin_y_mm`, `cpl_flip_y`, `cpl_rotation_cw`, `cpl_rotation_offset_deg` and `cpl_bottom_rotation`.
- **Names of footprints by IPC-7351B:** `ipcname::IpcName` writes and `ipcname::parse` reads the public convention: a chip as `RESC1608X55N` (the body in tenths of a millimetre, the height in hundredths, the density `L`, `N` or `M`), a part with leads on two sides as `SOIC127P600X175-8N` and one with leads on four sides as `QFP50P1000X1000X160-48N`; a size that cannot be written or a name that cannot be read is an error with the code of a bad row, and a name read and written again is the same name. Only the convention of the names is used: no table of land patterns is held.
- **`ipc-name`:** `phoenix-pnp ipc-name NAME... [--density L|N|M]` takes the names of the convention apart and writes them the one way (`--density` changes the density), names a chip package of the table (`R_0603`, `C0402`, `LED-1206`) from the size that `package-gen` knows, and `ipc-name --make FAMILY --pitch MM --span MM --length MM --width MM --height MM --pins N` makes a name from the sizes. The exit code is 1 when a name is not understood.
- **Footprint names brought to one form:** `fpsearch::key` makes the names of one footprint alike: the library before a colon and the metric size of KiCad go, and `R_0603`, `Resistor_SMD:R_0603_1608Metric`, `RESC1608X55N` and `r0603` are one chip body of one kind, while `C_0603` and `R_0805` are not; `0603` with no kind in front matches the chips of that body of any kind.
- **`footprint-find`:** `phoenix-pnp footprint-find QUERY... [--packages F] [--catalog F] [--limit N] [--distance N]` looks a name up in the table of packages and lists the same text first, then another name of the same footprint, then the names that hold the words, then the names a few letters away, each name once (`fpsearch::search`). `--limit` and `--distance` default to the settings `ipc_search_limit` and `ipc_search_distance`; `ipc_density` is the density of the names that `ipc-name` makes. The exit code is 1 when a query finds nothing.
- **`dfm-check`:** `phoenix-pnp dfm-check PARTS FEATURES [--packages F] [--catalog F] [--out F]` judges a board for assembly from the parts, the table of packages and a table of features (`kind,id,x_mm,y_mm,w_mm,h_mm,side` with the outline, pads, holes, ends of tracks, fiducials, tooling holes and tabs, `examples/features.csv`): pads of one part at a fine pitch (a note), the gap between the pads of two parts, the space between the bodies of the parts on the surface (a part that goes through the board is left out), a hole inside a pad that is soldered (an error) and a track that ends in the middle of a pad. The exit code is 1 on a warning or an error (`dfm::check`).
- **The rules of the panel in `dfm-check`:** the diameter of a tooling hole and its distance from the edge of the outline, the parts that are too near the edge that has the tooling holes, the diameter of a fiducial, its distance from the edge and the ring around it that is kept free of pads and holes, and the width of a tab; a table with fiducials, tooling holes or tabs and no outline says that the checks of the edges were left out. A figure exactly on its limit keeps the limit.
- **The report of the checks in eight languages:** every finding has its message in English, Spanish, German, French, Italian, Japanese, Chinese and European Portuguese (`--lang`), the CSV of `--out` carries the message in that language, and the report says that the limits are the public figures of assembly guides and have not been checked against the rules of a house for as long as the setting `dfm_verified` is off.
- **`stencil-apertures`:** `phoenix-pnp stencil-apertures FEATURES [--out F]` makes the openings of the paste stencil from the pads of the table of features: each side smaller by `stencil_reduction_percent`, more by `stencil_fine_reduction_percent` for the pads of a part whose pitch is at or under `stencil_fine_pitch_mm`, and a pad whose two sides are at least `stencil_thermal_side_mm` split into windows (none longer than `stencil_window_max_mm`) that cover `stencil_thermal_percent` of it. `--out` writes the openings; the exit code is 1 on a warning (`stencil::make`).
- **The checks of the stencil:** the thickness, the reduction, the reduction for a fine pitch and the cover of a thermal pad are told when they are out of their ranges (`stencil_thickness_min_mm` to `stencil_thickness_max_mm`, `stencil_reduction_min_percent` to `stencil_reduction_max_percent`, `stencil_fine_reduction_min_percent` to `stencil_fine_reduction_max_percent`, `stencil_thermal_min_percent` to `stencil_thermal_max_percent`), and so are a web between windows narrower than `stencil_web_mm` and an opening whose area ratio, its area over the area of its walls (`L*W / (2*(L+W)*T)`), is under `stencil_area_ratio_min`; the messages are in the eight languages.
- **`sim-busy`:** `phoenix-pnp sim-busy JOURNAL [--out F]` reads the journal that `simulate --journal` writes and says how busy each nozzle and each feeder was (the seconds of its steps, the steps and the share of the time of the job), how many steps did not end well and where the machine waited, the `busy_gaps_listed` longest waits first (`busy::read_journal`, `busy::analyse`, `examples/journal.csv`). A damaged row is an error with its line. The exit code is 1 when a step did not end well.
- **`sim-tower`:** `phoenix-pnp sim-tower JOURNAL [--out F]` works out the light a signal tower would show at each second of a simulated job: red during a step that did not end well, yellow during a step that took more than `tower_slow_factor` times the median step of its kind and during a wait over `tower_idle_gap_s`, green the rest; intervals that touch and mean the same are joined and the seconds of the three lights add up to the time of the job (`tower::timeline`). The exit code is 1 when the tower was red.
- **New keys of the settings:** `fab_dnp_mode`, `fab_dnp_column`, `fab_tht_words`, `cpl_origin_x_mm`, `cpl_origin_y_mm`, `cpl_flip_y`, `cpl_rotation_cw`, `cpl_rotation_offset_deg`, `cpl_bottom_rotation`, `ipc_density`, `ipc_search_limit`, `ipc_search_distance`, `dfm_verified`, twelve limits `dfm_*` (eleven in millimetres and `dfm_track_center_frac`), seventeen `stencil_*` figures, `tower_idle_gap_s`, `tower_slow_factor` and `busy_gaps_listed`, each with its range (a value outside it is refused with `PHOENIX-PNP-006` and names the key), its line explained in `examples/machine.cfg` and a default that changes nothing for a file that does not set them.
- **Tests by chance and of the files for assembly:** `tests/fab.rs` (designators, the placement list and the bill, the dialects, the conversion undone on boards made by chance), `tests/ipc.rs` (names written and read on a fixed seed, the search), `tests/dfm.rs` (every rule, the eight languages, the stencil), `tests/simviews.rs` (the journal read back from `journal::csv`, the use of the nozzles, the tower covering the whole job on journals made by chance), `tests/fabcli.rs` (each command as a person runs it with the example files) and `tests/fabfuzz.rs` (every new reader given the example files damaged on a fixed seed, the order of designators checked as a total order, ranges read back whatever the order, a placement list read back, a name written the same after being read). 1532 tests in all.
- **Known limits told:** the README says that the readers of the position files of the CAD programs and of the houses, the files written for the houses, the names by IPC-7351B and the figures of the checks for assembly and of the stencil have not been compared with real files, with the standard or with the rules of a house.

## [0.1.2] - Server panel in the window, signed serial traceability, shift plans, several heads, footprint and clearance checks and rejection reports

- **The link to the EH3D server is the common client of the suite:** the commands of the server talk through `eh3d-serverclient` of EH3D-COMMON instead of an HTTP stack of their own, so a sibling folder `EH3D-COMMON` is needed to build (the CI and the release build take it at its tag, and the manifest says `depends_on`). A request that fails on the network is tried again with growing pauses (never a `POST` that may have been sent), the answers are read up to 1 MiB, the token is never printed, and a refusal of the server is told by its code in the language of the command (`--lang`) when the table of the client has the code, with the code and the detail of the server beside it. The keys that were there are the same; `server_retries` (0 to 5, 2 by default) is how many more times the link tries a request and `server_mdns` (`off` by default) is the only way the link may look for the server on the local network. `server_attempts` stays for the server in memory, and `server_timeout_s` is rounded up to whole seconds for the link. `HttpTransport` is gone; `server::ScLink` is the link and `server::connect` and `server::target_for` make it from the settings.
- **`server-feeders`:** `phoenix-pnp server-feeders [list | set SLOT PART COUNT | clear SLOT] [--sim] [--out F]` reads the feeders that the `pnp` machine of the server holds, with where each is picked from and its calibration (`GET .../feeders`), declares or refills one slot at once with no job (`PUT .../feeders`) or empties it, and prints the table after the change; `--out` writes it as a CSV, never over a file that exists. A machine that is running a job refuses a change, a slot, a name or a count that the plugin would refuse is refused before the request is made, and the exit code is 1 when a feeder holds no parts.
- **`server-calibrate`:** `phoenix-pnp server-calibrate feeder|nozzle|camera TARGET [--offset X,Y] [--timeout S] [--sim]` makes a calibration job on the machine of the server, starts it and follows it to its end. The target of a feeder is its slot and the one of a nozzle or a camera is a name; the offset (up to 5 mm on each axis) is the one the command brings, because the machine of the server measures nothing today, and for a feeder the table is printed afterwards so that the calibration can be read. The exit code is 1 when the job did not end `done`.
- **`server-send --dry-run`:** the `place` request is sent with `dry_run`, so that the server goes through the job without taking a part off a feeder and without counting a placement; the server in memory keeps the same rule.
- **The server in memory knows the new routes:** `server::SimServer` answers `GET` and `PUT .../feeders` (slots from 1 to 200, the same refusals, `machine_busy` while a job runs), the `calibrate` request (the offset of a feeder stays with its slot and moves the place it is picked from) and the dry run, so that each of them is tried without a network.
- **The Server machine panel:** a panel of the Tools tab (`--tool server`) that does, one line for each thing and in order, what the commands above do on the machine of the server: `list`, `set SLOT PART COUNT`, `clear SLOT`, `calibrate feeder|nozzle|camera TARGET [X,Y]`, `load` (the loaded table of feeders goes on the machine) and `dry-run` (the loaded board is placed as a dry run). It gives what was done and the table of feeders after the last line, writes `server_feeders.csv` without going over a file, says the line it stopped at, and has a box for the simulated server. It is in the eight languages.
- **`nozzle-zcal`:** `phoenix-pnp nozzle-zcal [FILE | --simulate] [--nozzles N] [--probes N] [--noise MM] [--spread MM] [--seed S] [--tol MM] [--out F] [--readings-out F]` takes several probings of the bed with each nozzle tip (`nozzle,z_mm`, `examples/z_probe.csv`) and gives the mean height of each tip, how much its probings differ, its offset from the mean of the tips and the correction, and calls out the tips that are out of line (over `nozzle_z_tol_mm`, 0.02 mm by default), noisy or probed once (`nozzlez::analyse`). With `--simulate` the heights of the tips and the probings are made up from a seed and the offsets that were found are compared with the heights that were made up. The exit code is 1 when a tip needs a look. It tests the arithmetic on a simulated head, not a real one.
- **`panel-fids`:** `phoenix-pnp panel-fids PANEL [--x-out P2,P5] [--min-sep MM] [--out F]` lists the marks of every board of a panel (the parts of its file that are marks, such as `FID1`) in the coordinates of the panel, with the place and the turn of the board counted in, and says which boards cannot be aligned by their own marks: a board with none, with only one, or whose two farthest marks are closer than `panel_fid_min_sep_mm` (10 mm by default) (`panelfids::analyse`, `examples/panel_marks.json`). A board marked as bad is left out with its marks, `--out` writes a row for each mark, and the exit code is 1 when a board cannot be aligned.
- **`tape-steps`:** `phoenix-pnp tape-steps PARTS FEEDERS [--out F] [--partial]` walks the steps of the plan and, for every pick, says which part of the reel it is, how many are left, the pick place and how far it is from the pick before on the same feeder, and marks the steps that are `low` (the first under the warning of the feeder), `last`, `empty`, off `pitch` (a strip must advance by its pitch, within `tape_pitch_tol_mm`, 0.05 mm by default), out of `reach` of the machine or of a feeder the table does not have (`tapesteps::walk`). `--out` writes every step, never over a file that exists or one that was read, and the exit code is 1 when a step is a fault.
- **`shift-oee`:** `phoenix-pnp shift-oee PARTS FEEDERS [--runs N] [--seed S] [--shift MINUTES] [--reel-change SECONDS] [--min-oee PERCENT] [--out F]` runs the plan on the simulated machine many times and gives the availability (the shift less the changes of reels that its boards need), the performance (the ideal time of the plan against the mean time of the runs that went to the end), the quality (the share of the runs that placed every part), the boards that fit in the shift and the overall effectiveness, the product of the three (`shiftoee::compute`). The shift is `shift_minutes` (480 by default) and a change of reel takes `reel_change_s` (60 by default). The exit code is 1 when the effectiveness is under `--min-oee`. It is a figure for the plan and the settings on a simulated machine, not for a real shift.
- **`import-assembler`:** `phoenix-pnp import-assembler FILE [--format cpl|kicad-pos|generic] [--unit mm|inch] [--out F]` reads the position files that assemblers and other programs write: the designator column called `Designator`, `Reference` or `RefDes`; the middle of the part as `Mid X`, `Center-X(mil)` or `PosX`; the layer as `Top`, `T`, `TopLayer` or `Bottom`; and numbers that carry their unit (`12.500mm`, `0.492in`, `490mil`) or whose unit is in the name of the column (`asmimport::parse`, `examples/altium_pnp.csv`). It says which shape it found and gives the parts in millimetres and degrees in a shape every command reads. What `export-assembler` writes is read back, and a row that cannot be read is an error with its line.
- **`bom-merge`:** `phoenix-pnp bom-merge FILE[:BOARDS]... [--max-rows N] [--out F]` adds the bills of materials of several boards, each for the number of boards that is wanted, joins the rows that are the same part (the value written alike, the package, the part number) and says which bill each part is for (`bommerge::merge`, `examples/bom_b.csv`). `--out` writes the list as a table, never over a file that exists or over a file that was read.
- **New keys of the settings:** `server_retries`, `server_mdns`, `nozzle_z_tol_mm`, `tape_pitch_tol_mm`, `shift_minutes`, `reel_change_s` and `panel_fid_min_sep_mm`, each with its range, its line explained in `examples/machine.cfg` and a default that changes nothing for a file that does not set them.
- **Tests by chance and of the new commands:** `tests/flow2.rs` checks, on boards made by chance from a fixed seed, that what `export-assembler` writes is read back by `import-assembler` in millimetres and in inches, that the offsets of the nozzles find the heights that were made up, that the effectiveness of a shift is the product of its three shares, and that the new readers never panic on the example files damaged in many ways and on bytes made by chance; `tests/server.rs` tries the link with a transport that fails the first times (the number of tries follows `server_retries`), the refusals told in other languages, the new routes of the server in memory and the new commands against the loopback address.
- **Where the build looks for EH3D-COMMON:** the Rust CI and the release build check out the sibling repositories named in `depends_on` of the manifest next to this one, at the tag that `SIBLING_REFS` gives, as the other programs of the suite do.
- **The server panel of the window:** the Machine tab ends with the panel of `eh3d-view` of EH3D-COMMON, the same one that the other programs of the suite show: the settings of the server (address, token, machine, time of a try, retries, looking on the network), the state of the link, the test of the connection and the login and logout of a person with a name and a password (the program is `phoenix-pnp` for the server, and the password is wiped as soon as the call is made). Its words come with it, in the languages of the suite. What is applied goes to the configuration, `mode` follows the address (a server gives `server`, none gives `local`) and the keys are kept in the file of settings that was loaded; the token is never written to a file by the window (`gui::serverui`, `toolvalues::keep_lines`).
- **The six commands of the flow have a panel in the Tools tab:** `nozzle-zcal` (Nozzle heights, with the probings typed or made up on the simulated machine and the tolerance), `panel-fids` (Marks of the panel, of the panel that is loaded), `tape-steps` (Tapes by step), `shift-oee` (Shift effectiveness, with the runs of the Runs tool and the shift and the change of a reel typed), `import-assembler` (Assembler file, with the shape and the unit it is given back in) and `bom-merge` (Several bills, two bills with the boards of each). Each calls the same function as its command and gives the same text, and each has its name, its hint and its labels in the eight languages (`gui::toolmore`, `gui::toolbox`).
- **`server_attempts` is the old name of `server_retries`:** a file of settings that has `server_attempts` and not `server_retries` is read with the retries one fewer than the attempts (up to 5), and every command that reads such a file says in a note that the key has a new name. When both are written nothing is said and `server_retries` wins; the attempts still count for the server in memory.
- **`server-discover` and the feature `mdns`:** `phoenix-pnp server-discover [--sim]` lists the servers that answer on the local network. It asks nothing of the network unless `server_mdns = on` is in the settings, and a build with `--features mdns` brings the resolver of the common client (UDP, no more dependencies); a build without it says so instead of failing in silence. `--sim` answers with one server in memory, so that the rest can be tried without a network (`server::discover`).
- **`job-fingerprint`:** `phoenix-pnp job-fingerprint FILE...` prints the SHA-256 of each table and one for the set, after making the line endings and the spaces at the end of a line alike, so that the same table saved on Windows and on Linux has the same fingerprint (`serialsig::fingerprint`, `sha256`).
- **`serial-sign`:** `phoenix-pnp serial-sign PARTS FEEDERS --serial S [--lots F] [--prev SIG] [--key-file F] [--out F] [--partial]` writes the record of one board by its serial: the fingerprints of the tables it was made from, each placed reference with its feeder and the lot of its reel, and a signature of all of it, a SHA-256 or, with the key of a file, an HMAC-SHA-256. `--prev` puts the signature of the board before in the record, so that the records of a run form a chain in which nothing can be taken out or changed unseen. The key is never printed, a board with a part that no feeder holds is refused unless `--partial` says so, and the record is never written over a file that exists or over a table that was read (`serialsig`, `examples/lots.csv`).
- **`serial-verify`:** `phoenix-pnp serial-verify FILE [--key-file F] [--prev SIG]` reads a record and says whether it is whole: the shape, the counts and the signature. A record with a byte changed, a line taken out or added, the wrong key, no key where one is needed or a key where none was used does not pass, and with `--prev` it must name the board before. The exit code is 1 when it is not whole.
- **`shift-plan`:** `phoenix-pnp shift-plan PARTS FEEDERS [--boards N] [--shift MINUTES] [--reel-change SECONDS] [--out F] [--partial]` runs the shift board by board on the plan: a board takes the time of the plan and, from each feeder, the parts the plan takes, and a feeder that cannot supply the next board has its reel changed for a full one of the same size. It says at which board and at which minute each change falls, how many boards the shift holds, how much of it the changes take and the boards an hour; the first changes are listed and the table of all of them goes to `--out` (`shiftplan`).
- **`head-split`:** `phoenix-pnp head-split PARTS FEEDERS [--heads N] [--out F]` plays the board on a machine with up to 8 heads that take from the same feeders, a reel being picked from by one head at a time: the board cut across into bands against whole feeders to a head. It says how long each head works and waits, when the board is done, how much faster that is than one head and which feeders are shared (`headsplit`).
- **`footprint-check`:** `phoenix-pnp footprint-check PARTS [--packages F] [--catalog F] [--out F]` says how each package of the board stands against the table of packages: known, an alias of a row under another name (`Resistor_SMD:R_0603_1608Metric` for `R_0603`, taking the row of the same kind of part), made from its name as `package-gen` does, a mistake away from a row, or unknown. The exit code is 1 when a package is similar or unknown (`footcheck`).
- **`reject-report`:** `phoenix-pnp reject-report PARTS FEEDERS REJECTS [--boards N] [--max-rate PERCENT] [--out F]` reads the parts rejected in a run (`reference,reason[,board]`, `examples/rejects.csv`) and gives the reasons in the order of a Pareto chart with their running share, the packages and feeders that reject most for what they placed, and the references rejected on the largest share of the boards. A reference that is not a part of the board is told; the exit code is 1 when a reference reaches `--max-rate` (`rejects`).
- **`retry-policy`:** `phoenix-pnp retry-policy PARTS FEEDERS RULES [--out F]` gives each part the feed, pick and placement retries of the first rule of a table (`field,pattern,...` with `reference`, `value`, `package` or `feeder` as the field and `*` and `?` in the pattern, `examples/retry_rules.csv`), the settings giving the rest, and works out the chance of a good board and the seconds of retries. It says which rules fit no part and which never act because an earlier rule took every part they fit, and exits with 1 for them (`retrypolicy`, `failodds::part_odds`).
- **`clearance`:** `phoenix-pnp clearance PARTS --packages F [--catalog F] [--min MM] [--out F]` finds the pairs of parts on the same side whose bodies, from the table of packages and turned with the part, are nearer than `--min` or overlap, the nearest first; the parts without a body size are told. The exit code is 1 when there is a pair (`clearance`).
- **`bom-cost`:** `phoenix-pnp bom-cost BOM PRICES [--boards N] [--spare PERCENT] [--currency TEXT] [--max-rows N] [--out F]` costs a run from the bill of materials and a table of prices with price breaks (`part_number|value[,package],price[,qty_from]`, `examples/prices.csv`): the pieces of each part with the spares, the break they fall in, the lowest quantity that is bought when fewer are needed (and how many that is above the need), the cost of the run and of a board, and the parts with no price, for which the exit code is 1 (`bomcost`).
- **`lot-age`:** `phoenix-pnp lot-age PARTS FEEDERS LOTS --week YYWW [--max-weeks N] [--out F]` says how old the lot in each feeder that gives parts is, from the date code of the table of lots (`YYWW` or `YYYYWW`), against the week that is given, never read from a clock, and the limit. A lot that is old, from the future, without a readable code or missing is a reason for the exit code 1 (`lotage`).
- **New keys of the settings:** `clearance_min_mm` (0 to 5, 0.2 by default) and `lot_max_weeks` (1 to 1040, 104 by default), each with its range and its line explained in `examples/machine.cfg`.
- **Tests by chance and of the shop floor:** `tests/shop.rs` runs each new command as a person does with the example files (signing and checking records, changing them byte by byte, keys, chains, shifts, heads, footprints, rejects, rules, costs, lots, the network and the old key), `tests/toolshop.rs` the six panels, `tests/serverui.rs` the panel of the server on a server in memory, and `tests/flow3.rs` damages the example files on a fixed seed for every new reader, reads back records made of anything, and checks on boards and shifts made by chance that a longer shift never holds fewer boards, that the bodies are judged the same in any order and place, that the costs and the rejects add up, and that the heads never beat the work shared out. The hash is checked against the published vectors.

## [0.1.1] - Server mode, camera self-test, machine templates, lot tracing, assembler export, job splitting and failure odds

- **Settings for the EH3D server:** `mode` (`local`, the default, or `server`), `server_address` (`host:port`), `server_token`, `server_machine` (an id of letters, digits, `_` and `-`), `server_timeout_s` (0.5 to 120), `server_poll_ms` (50 to 60000) and `server_attempts` (1 to 10) are keys of `machine.cfg`, each with its range and its line in `examples/machine.cfg` (a value outside it is refused with `PHOENIX-PNP-005`, the line named). The address, the token and the machine are empty until given, and nothing talks to a server unless `mode = server` and a command of the server is run: the local mode is not touched.
- **`server-send`:** `phoenix-pnp server-send PARTS FEEDERS [--offset X,Y] [--keep-feeders] [--start] [--follow] [--timeout S] [--sim] [--partial]` sends the table of feeders (the switched-on feeders in slots 1, 2, ... of the machine) and the placements to the `pnp` machine of the EH3D server as jobs of the generic queue (`/api/v1/work/jobs`, `kind` `load_feeders` and `place`), each part by the name of the feeder that holds its value and package (`server::feeder_rows`, `server::placements`). It waits queued until `--start`; `--follow` reads the job until it is over and prints the state and each tenth of the work. A part that no feeder holds is told and stops the command unless `--partial` leaves it out. The exit code is 1 when the job did not end `done`. What the real plugin does with a job on a real machine has not been tried: the requests follow its documented contract.
- **`server-status` and `server-job`:** `phoenix-pnp server-status [JOB]` shows the machine of the settings (its state and the feeders the server holds, with the parts left) or how a job goes, and `phoenix-pnp server-job ID --start|--pause|--resume|--cancel` tells a job what to do. Both need `mode = server`; a request that gets no answer is tried again up to `server_attempts` times, and an answer (a refusal too) is final.
- **A server in memory:** `server::SimServer` answers the same routes with the rules of the plugin (slots from 1 to 200, names of up to 64 letters, digits and `_-./+:`, counts up to 100000, a reference once, the stock of every part, a feeder that runs dry fails the job) and carries a job out part by part, so that `server-send --sim` and the tests of the server need no network; `server::HttpTransport` is plain HTTP/1.1 (one request for each connection, answers up to 1 MiB, chunked bodies put together) and the tests run it against a small server on the loopback address.
- **The token is never shown:** `config-diff` shows `(set)` for `server_token` instead of its value, and the commands of the server print the address of an unreachable server and never the token.
- **`server-request`:** `phoenix-pnp server-request PARTS FEEDERS --out DIR [--offset X,Y] [--partial]` writes the two requests that `server-send` would make, `feeders.json` and `place.json`, for other programs that talk to the server; nothing is sent, no server is needed and a folder that has the files is never written over.
- **`camera-selftest`:** `phoenix-pnp camera-selftest [--points N] [--noise PX] [--field PX] [--seed S] [--tol MM] [--out F]` makes up the camera of the settings (the `camera_*` keys), looks at a mark in places chosen by chance from the seed (the `sim_seed` of the settings by default), adds the noise of a reading, fits the camera as `camera-calibrate` does and says how far the camera that was found is from the one that was made up over a grid of the picture (`calibsim::selftest`), so that the number of sightings and the noise that a calibration can bear are known before a machine exists. `--out` writes the sightings as the file `camera-calibrate` reads. The exit code is 1 when the error is over `--tol` (0.05 mm by default). It is a test of the fit on a simulated camera, not of a real one.
- **`machine-template`:** `phoenix-pnp machine-template [NAME] [--out F]` lists the starting points for the settings of a machine (`desktop-1`, `desktop-2`, `dual-head`, `production-4`, `simulation`, `server`, `full`) or prints or writes one (`template::find`): a few lines that say what differs from the defaults, with a comment that says why (`full` is the documented file with every key). Every template is read by the same reader as any settings file, a test reads them all, and the numbers are starting points to tune on the machine. A file that exists is never written over.
- **`lot-trace`:** `phoenix-pnp lot-trace PARTS FEEDERS LOTS [--serial S] [--out F]` says for each placed part which reel and which lot it came from, with a table `feeder,lot[,reel][,date_code][,supplier]` of what is in the feeders (`examples/lots.csv`, `lottrace::parse_lots`, `lottrace::trace`): a line for each lot with its parts, a CSV with a row for each part (the serial of the board in the first column), the feeders that give parts and have no lot, the parts no feeder holds and the lots that name a feeder that is not in the table. The exit code is 1 when a part has no feeder or a feeder that gives parts has no lot.
- **`export-assembler`:** `phoenix-pnp export-assembler PARTS [--format cpl|kicad-pos|generic] [--side all|top|bottom] [--unit mm|inch] [--out F]` writes the parts in the shape that an assembler or another program asks for (`asmexport::write`): the component placement list (designator, value, package, mid x, mid y, rotation, layer, the places with their unit), the text file with columns that KiCad writes and `parts` reads back, or a plain CSV with the unit in the name of its columns. The parts come in the order of their references, the turn is always from 0 up to 360, and what is written is read back to the digits that are written (a test does it for boards made by chance).
- **`split-job`:** `phoenix-pnp split-job PARTS FEEDERS --machines N [--out F]` gives whole feeders to N machines (up to 16) so that each has about the same work, the feeders with the most parts first, each to the machine that has the least so far (`splitjob::split`): a reel is on one machine, so the parts of a feeder go together, and the same board and tables always give the same split. The work is the time of a pick and a placement of the settings; the moves of the head are left out. `--out` writes a row `machine,feeder,reference` for each part. The exit code is 1 when a part has no feeder.
- **`failure-odds`:** `phoenix-pnp failure-odds PARTS FEEDERS [--rules F] [--min-ok PERCENT] [--out F]` says, with arithmetic and no chance, how likely a board is to go through with no part in error for the retries that are allowed (`failodds::analyse`): a stage that fails with chance `p` and may be tried `r + 1` times ends in error with chance `p^(r+1)`, using `sim_feed_fail_rate`, `sim_pick_fail_rate`, `sim_place_fail_rate`, `sim_part_fail` and `sim_feeder_fail`; it gives the failures and retries expected for each package, the seconds the retries take and the chance of a good board. A file of rules `pattern,feed_retries,pick_retries,place_retries` (`examples/failure_rules.csv`) gives other retries to the packages that match, to compare the retries of a small part and of a large one (the job itself still takes the retries of the settings). The exit code is 1 when the chance is under `--min-ok` (0 by default: never).
- **`pos-transform`:** `phoenix-pnp pos-transform PARTS [--rotate DEG] [--flip] [--shift X,Y] [--zero] [--format F] [--unit U] [--out F]` turns the whole table counter-clockwise about the origin, flips it left to right (the sides swap and the angles mirror), moves it, and with `--zero` moves it so that its lowest left corner is the origin (`reorient::apply`), for a board that lies turned on the machine or is placed from its other side. The distances between the parts do not change and a turn and its opposite, or two flips, give the table back; a table that would go beyond a million millimetres is refused.
- **Chance on a fixed seed:** `tests/fuzz.rs` gives every reader of a file (parts, feeders, settings, panels, jobs in a file, recipes, camera sightings, lots, rules, refills, catalogs) the example files damaged in many ways and bytes made by chance, and the reader of server answers answers cut or mixed up, and none may panic; and boards made by chance keep the properties the tools promise: what is written is read back, a transform keeps the distances and is undone, a split gives every part once, the odds stay between 0 and 1 and do not fall when a retry is allowed, a trace names a lot for every part and a request that is made is taken by the server in memory. The numbers are the same on every run and a failure names its seed.
- **Every key of the settings is tested:** `tests/keys.rs` checks that the keys are different names in lower case, that the example gives every key and only the keys of the list, that each is known to the reader with the value of the example, that only the keys that take a name or a list take any text (a number that is too big is refused by every other key), that a key written twice is refused unless it is a list of rules, and that a key that is not in the list is refused with its line, so a key added without its check or its line in the example fails a test.
- **Every command is tried with nothing, a wrong option and missing files:** `tests/cli_all.rs` runs each command that the help names with no argument, with an option it does not have and with files that are not there, and asks for an error line and the exit code 1 (or success when it needs nothing) and never a panic, and checks that the help names every command of the program and every command of the help is one the program knows.
- **The values of the forms are settings of the machine file:** `parts_diff_tol_mm` (0 to 100) and `parts_diff_rot_tol_deg` (0 to 180) for `parts-diff`, `board_stats_near_mm` (0.01 to 50; 0 leaves the look off) and `board_stats_limit` (1 to 1000) for `board-stats`, `feeder_slots_max_plans` (1 to 1000000) for `feeder-slots`, `simulate_runs_count` (1 to 10000) for `simulate-runs` and `simulate-hotspots`, and `journal_steps` (0 to 1000000; 0 runs the job to its end) for the journal panel. Each has its range (a value outside it is refused with `PHOENIX-PNP-006`), its line explained in `examples/machine.cfg`, and as its default the value the program had before, so nothing changes for a file that does not set them. A command takes them when its option is not given and the option is stronger.
- **The panels start with those values and can keep them:** the forms of the Tools tab (the tolerances of the parts diff, the distance and the pairs of the board statistics, the plans of the feeder places, the runs and the journal steps) take their starting values from the machine file and reload them when another file is loaded. A button keeps what was typed in the file that was loaded last (`toolvalues::keep`): only the lines of these keys change (or are added at the end), the comments, the other lines and the line endings stay as they are, the file is written to a file beside it and moved into place, and a value that the file would refuse, a file that is not valid or one that is not there leaves everything as it was and says why.
- **A batch file may be a hook:** a program named `.bat` or `.cmd` is no longer refused in the rules of `simulate --hooks` (`hooks::is_batch`, `hooks::warnings`). The rules still name only a file of the folder of the rules, with a name of letters, digits, `.`, `_` and `-`, and hooks are still off until `hooks` is on, but each batch file that the rules name is told once before the job runs (and also in `--hooks-dry-run`), because it runs commands of the system with the arguments of its rule; the time limit, the limit of what is printed and the limit of programs for a job are the same as for any program.
- **`reel-runway`:** `phoenix-pnp reel-runway PARTS FEEDERS [--boards N] [--out F]` says how many whole boards the reel of each switched-on feeder lasts, how many parts are left over after them and which reel runs out first (`runway::runway`): the parts that one board takes from a feeder come from the plan, a feeder that no part takes from lasts for ever and is listed last, and a feeder that is off is not listed. The exit code is 1 when a reel lasts fewer than N boards (1 when `--boards` is not given), and `--out` writes the table without going over a file.

The exit code is 1 when no number gives a plan.

- **`job-diff`:** `phoenix-pnp job-diff FIRST SECOND [--out F]` says what differs between two files of `job-export` (`jobdiff::diff`): the parts that are in one and not in the other, the feeder, the nozzle, the batch and the tip of a part, where it is picked and where it is placed (within a thousandth of a millimetre they are the same place), its turn (within a hundredth of a degree, the short way round), what became of it in the job and why, the order of the parts that are in both, and the plan as a whole (the order strategy, the batches, the tip changes, the tiers, the travel, the seconds and the phase). `--out` writes the differences as a table, never over a file that exists or over a file that was read, a file that is not a job is named with its code, and the exit code is 1 when the jobs differ.
- **`feeder-slots`:** `phoenix-pnp feeder-slots PARTS FEEDERS [--max-plans N] [--out F]` looks for the places of the feeders that make the plan the fastest (`slots::optimize`): it swaps the places of two feeders of the same kind (tape with tape, tube with tube, bulk with bulk, both switched on; a strip and a tray stay where they are), plans the board again with each swap and keeps the swap that saves the most, until none saves time or `--max-plans` (5000 by default) plans were made. The report gives each feeder that moves, with its slot name when the table has one, and the seconds and the travel before and after; the table with the new places keeps all its other columns and its delimiter and goes to `--out` (never over a file that exists or over a file that was read). The result is the same for the same input, the feeders only trade places, and whether a tape fits the slot it is moved to is left to the person. The exit code is 1 when a better way to stand the feeders exists.

It measures the plan and the settings on a simulated machine, not a machine.

- **Hooks, off by default:** `simulate --hooks FILE` runs a program of the operator for the events of a job (`job-finished`, `job-paused`, `job-stopped`, `part-aside` for each part put aside, `feeder-off` for each feeder switched off), to light a lamp, play a sound or send a message. - **What a hook may be:** the rules file (`event,program,argument...`, `examples/hooks.csv`) is the list of what may be run. A program is a file in the folder of the rules, named with letters, digits, `.`, `_` and `-`: no path, no search of the system, no shell, no link out of the folder, no `.bat` or `.cmd`. The arguments are passed one by one, with `{event}`, `{reference}`, `{feeder}`, `{reason}`, `{phase}`, `{placed}` and `{total}` filled in once (data that looks like a name between brackets is not filled in again). A rules file is at most 64 KiB and 50 rules, a rule has at most 8 arguments of 256 characters without control characters, and a rule that is not safe is refused with its line before the job runs (`PHOENIX-PNP-009`).
- **Limits on running:** at most `hooks_max_runs` (20) programs for a job, `hooks_timeout_s` (5) seconds for each before it is stopped, what it prints cut to 4096 bytes, no window of its own on Windows. A hook that fails, is not found or is stopped is told and changes neither the job nor the exit code. New settings: `hooks` (off), `hooks_timeout_s` and `hooks_max_runs`.

The symmetry of the part counts (a rectangle looks the same every 180 degrees, a square every 90), the sides come in order whichever way a reading gives them, and `Measured::from_reading` takes a reading of the vision pipeline and the size of a pixel. `phoenix-pnp part-inspect READINGS --sizes SIZES [--out F]` reads a CSV `ref,package,long_mm,short_mm,dx_mm,dy_mm,rot_deg[,exp_rot_deg]` and the table of sizes `package,long_mm,short_mm` (a name may hold `*` and `?`, an exact name wins), writes `ref,package,verdict,...` and exits with 1 while a part is refused or has no size to be judged against (`examples/package_sizes.csv`, `examples/nozzle_reads.csv`).
- **Settings of the look:** `part_size_tol_mm` (0.01 to 5, default 0.3), `part_angle_tol_deg` (0.1 to 45, default 5) and `part_offset_max_mm` (0.1 to 20, default 2).
- **What the fiducials say about the panel:** `fidcheck::decompose` takes the fit apart into its turn, the scale along each axis, how far the axes are from square and whether the plane is flipped over, and `fidcheck::check` judges it against `fiducial_max_rotation_deg` (default 5), `fiducial_scale_tol_percent` (default 1), `fiducial_shear_tol_deg` (default 0.5) and `fiducial_max_shift_mm` (default 5, the move of the middle of the marks); `check_levels` also judges each board against the turn of the panel (`fiducial_level_turn_deg`, default 2). `fiducial_check` is `off`, `warn` (the default: the findings are printed as `fiducial check:` lines) or `refuse` (a finding stops the job with the code `PHOENIX-PNP-032`).
- **The mark that does not agree:** `fidcheck::analyze_points` fits the marks again without each of them in turn and names the only one whose absence brings every other mark within `fiducial_max_residual_mm`, and says how much a fit with one scale and no shear would leave (how warped the panel is). With `fiducial_reject = drop_worst` (`refuse` by default) a fit that is over the limit because of that one mark is made again without it, only when the marks that are left still fix the fit more than they need (four marks for a similarity, five for an affine fit) and no other mark could be the one; the mark that was left out is said. `phoenix-pnp fiducial-check DESIGN MEASURED` (`id,x,y`) prints it all and exits with 1 when the residual or a limit is broken (`examples/fid_design.csv`, `examples/fid_measured.csv`).
- **The library of feeders checks itself:** `feedlib::check` reads the whole table of feeders and lists what does not agree, as errors (a slot given twice, a tray with more parts than cells, a pick place outside the area the machine reaches, also at the end of a strip, two pick places closer than `feeder_min_gap_mm`) and warnings (a strip whose pitch is not a whole number of `feeder_pitch_step_mm`, cells under half a millimetre apart, a feeder that is on and empty, a warning level over the capacity, a package that the catalog does not have). `phoenix-pnp feeder-check FEEDERS [--packages F] [--catalog F]` prints them and exits with 1 on an error. A stick of parts (a tube) is a tape feeder with one pick place.
- **Settings of the library:** `feeder_pitch_step_mm` (0 to 100, default 2, 0 does not check) and `feeder_min_gap_mm` (0 to 1000, default 3, 0 does not check).
- **The picks of each feeder in a job:** `feedrep::build` gives, for every feeder, the picks the plan asks of it and the seconds it keeps it busy, the picks that worked and the ones that failed (also those a retry put right), the share of failures, the faults and whether it was switched off; a feeder that failed more than `feeder_fail_percent` (default 20) of its picks, once it was tried `feeder_fail_min_picks` times (default 10), is marked. `simulate --report-feeders F` writes it as a CSV (a file that exists is never written over) and names the marked feeders in its output.

## [0.0.7] - Vacuum and actuators, several heads and the camera model, recipes of a job, the centroid import and panels with boards marked as bad

- **The simulated vacuum:** with `vacuum = on` the pump of a nozzle sucks for `vacuum_suck_s` seconds after every pick and the level of the nozzle is read in kilopascals; a level under `vacuum_pick_min_kpa` says that the nozzle holds no part, the part is let go of and the pick is a failure that the retries, `error_policy` and the decisions of the person handle like any other. After every place the nozzle blows for `vacuum_blow_s` seconds and is read again; a level over `vacuum_release_max_kpa` says that the part is still stuck to the nozzle, the nozzle holds it again and the place is a failure. Off by default: with `vacuum = off` a job (its plan, its events, its reports and its saved state) is exactly what it was, whatever the other vacuum settings say.
- **Settings of the vacuum:** `vacuum` (`off` or `on`), `vacuum_suck_s` and `vacuum_blow_s` (0 to 10 seconds, added to the clock of the machine and to the time of the pick and of the place), `vacuum_free_kpa`, `vacuum_held_kpa`, `vacuum_pick_min_kpa` and `vacuum_release_max_kpa` (0 to 101, which must satisfy free <= release_max < pick_min <= held or the file is refused as an unsafe setting); `examples/machine.cfg` documents them with the default of every one, and a test keeps the example equal to the defaults.
- **The simulated sensor:** a nozzle that holds a part reads `vacuum_held_kpa` and one that holds nothing reads `vacuum_free_kpa`, each with an error of up to `sim_vacuum_noise_kpa`; `sim_vacuum_miss_rate` and `sim_vacuum_stuck_rate` (0 to 1) are the chances that a pick leaves no part on the nozzle and that a place leaves the part stuck to it, and a test can force a given check (`SimVacuum::force_miss`, `force_stuck`). The sensor has a generator of its own drawn from `sim_seed`, so the failures of the picks, the places, the feeds and the camera do not move when it is used.
- **Statistics of the vacuum:** the checks after a pick and after a place, how many found no part or a stuck part, the lowest level that said a part was held and the highest that said it was gone, in the text of `simulate` (`vacuum: ...`), in the JSON report (`vacuum`, with the last level of every nozzle) and in the rows `vacuum` of `--report-csv`, which come before the section of the align step (the header does not change); a saved job keeps them, and a state saved with the vacuum belongs to the job with the vacuum.
- **On/off actuators:** `actuator = NAME : DELAY_S` (one line for each device, up to 32, a name of letters, digits, `_` and `-`) declares a valve, a light or a pump; `Job::set_actuator` switches it by name, adds its delay to the clock of the machine, notes it in the log and counts the switches, which are in the JSON report (`actuators`), in the rows `actuator` of the CSV and in the saved state. Library: `sim::SimActuator::{new, name, delay_s, switches}`, `Job::{actuators, set_actuator}`.
- **The window of faults of a feeder has an action:** `fault_action` is `disable` (what it always did: the feeder is switched off and another one of the same kind takes over) or `alert`: a feeder that reaches `fault_limit` faults among its last `fault_window` picks (a failed pick or a failed feed) stops the job instead, with the feeder, the number of faults and the window in the detail of the error.
- **Decisions when the window of faults stops the job:** the dialog of the Job tab and `Job::act` offer the four actions they already had, now for this stop: try the step again, leave the part out, put it aside or switch the feeder off. The first three also clear the faults of that feeder, so that the same faults do not stop the job again at once; switching the feeder off lets another feeder of the same kind take over. The Machine tab lists `fault_action` and `vacuum`.
- **New error codes:** `PHOENIX-PNP-044` (the vacuum shows no part on the nozzle after the pick), `PHOENIX-PNP-045` (the vacuum shows the part still on the nozzle after the place), `PHOENIX-PNP-046` (the machine has no actuator with that name) and `PHOENIX-PNP-047` (a feeder has too many faults among its last picks: the person decides), with their meaning in the eight languages and in the eight READMEs.
- **The model of a camera:** `camera::CameraModel` says how a picture relates to the machine: the size of a pixel on the part (`units_per_px`, 0 for the size the vision pipeline is set to), where the axis of the nozzle is in the picture (`offset_x_mm`, `offset_y_mm`), how much the picture is turned and whether it is mirrored, and how the lens differs from what the software believes (`lens_scale`, `lens_k1`). `to_pixels` is what the optics do with a true place, `to_machine` what the software believes a pixel is, `read` both one after the other, and `angle_to_image` and `angle_to_machine` carry the turn of a part through the same turn and mirror. The default model changes nothing.
- **Settings of the camera:** `camera_units_per_px` (0, or 0.001 to 10 millimetres), `camera_offset_x_mm` and `camera_offset_y_mm` (-500 to 500), `camera_rotation_deg` (-180 to 180), `camera_flip_x` and `camera_flip_y`, `camera_lens_scale` (0.5 to 2) and `camera_lens_k1` (-0.01 to 0.01 per square millimetre); `examples/machine.cfg` documents them with their defaults, which a test keeps equal to the defaults of the program.
- **The simulated camera reads through the model:** with `vision = off` the reading of a part is its place seen through the lens (a lens scale of 1.25 reads 1 mm as 0.8 mm, and the passes of the align step then correct what is left); with `vision = pipeline` the part is drawn where the optics put it, with the size in pixels that its true size gives (a camera whose pixel is five times larger than the one the pipeline is set for draws a spot too small to be the part), and what the pipeline measures goes back through the model to the frame of the machine. A camera with the default model, and a job without the align step, are exactly what they were. Library: `SimCamera::{model, set_model, for_head}`.
- **Heads:** `head = NAME : NOZZLES [: OFFSET_X : OFFSET_Y : CAMERA_X : CAMERA_Y : CAMERA_ROT : CAMERA_UNITS : CAMERA_LENS]` declares up to four heads, each with its nozzles (1 to 6), its offset with respect to the carriage and its camera; an empty or missing camera field is the one of the `camera_*` settings. A machine that declares none has one head with `nozzles` nozzles. Library: `config::HeadSpec` and `MachineConfig::head_list`.
- **The parts are shared out between the heads:** `heads::plan_heads` gives each part to a head that reaches both its place and its feeder within the limits of the machine (a head with an offset puts its nozzle over a place when the carriage is at that place less the offset), heaviest first, to the head that would finish it first, counting the change of tip for a head that has not used that tip yet; equal costs go to the first head, and the result does not depend on the order of the files. Each head is then planned as a machine of its own, in the coordinates of its carriage (`HeadPlan::board_xy` gives a place back in the coordinates of the board), and a machine with one head gets the plan it always had.
- **The clock counts the waits of the heads:** every plan records when its head arrives at each feeder and for how long the feeder is busy (`AdvancedPlan::pick_times`), and `heads::schedule` serves the heads in the order of their arrivals: a head that comes to a feeder that another head is using waits, and everything after that pick in the head moves later by the same time. The plan of the heads gives, for each head, the seconds of its plan alone, its waits and when it is done, and for the machine the time of the last head.
- **`schedule` with two or more heads:** the command prints a line for each head (its parts, its nozzles and its time with the waits) and the total, and the plan file gets a key `heads` with the same, the places in the coordinates of the board; with one head or none the file and the output are the ones they always were.
- **Nozzle tips that a package accepts:** `tip_compat = PATTERN : TIP, TIP, ...` (one line for each rule, up to 64 rules of up to eight tips, `*` and `?` in the pattern, the first rule that matches a package is the one used, the preferred tip first) and `tip_compat_mode` (`fewest_changes`, the default, or `preferred`). With `fewest_changes` every kind of part gets one tip of its list so that the machine needs as few different tips as it can (the tips that a kind accepts alone first, then the tip that covers the most parts, the preferred one among equals), which is as few changes of tip as it can; with `preferred` every kind takes the first tip of its list. A part whose row of the tables names a tip keeps it (`Catalog::explicit_tip`), and the search for the order of the tips works on the tips chosen. Without rules the tips are the ones of the tables and the defaults. Library: `tips::{TipRule, TipChoice, Need, choose, resolve, rule_for}`.

- **The Machine tab lists** the number of heads, the size of a pixel of the camera and the mode of the choice of tips.

A state saved before has neither and loads as it did.

## [0.0.6] - Job report and Job tab, simulated vision, alignment step, boards marked as bad and two-level fiducials

- **A picture mode for the window:** `--shot FILE.png` (built with `--features gui`, with the command line as `phoenix-pnp --shot FILE.png FILES...` or with `phoenix-pnp-gui`) opens the window at the size of `--size WxH` (320 to 8192, 1180x760 by default) with the files given (bare names or `--open FILE`) loaded, in the theme of `--theme phoenix|light|contrast` and the language of `--lang CODE` (English by default), waits `--wait N` frames (0 to 3600, 6 by default), saves what it drew as a PNG file and closes by itself. - **`phoenix-pnp tables-check`:** reads each table that is named (`--parts`, `--packages`, `--catalog`, `--feeders`, `--slots`, with `--config` and `--lang`), prints `ok:` with the number of rows or `not valid:` with the code and the row of the error in the language asked for, reads all of them before it stops, and exits with 1 when any is not valid.
- **Examples:** `examples/board_dnp.csv` (a board with a `dnp` column), `examples/feeders_kinds.csv` (a tape, a strip, a tray filled in a snake and a feeder that is switched off) and `examples/packages_common.csv` (a table of common packages to adjust to your own parts), all read by `tables-check` in a test.
- **`simulate --report-csv FILE`:** the statistics of the job as a CSV with the header `section,name,value,picked,pick_failures,placed,place_failures,faults,disabled` that never changes: the totals, a row for each package and each feeder, and a row for each part put aside with its reason; it is never written over a file the job was made from.
- **New error codes:** `PHOENIX-PNP-033` (a mark of mounting that is neither yes nor no, or a reference to leave out that is not known) and `PHOENIX-PNP-034` (the file of positions is not valid: every error of the position file now carries it, with the row or the line), with their meaning in the eight languages and in the eight READMEs.
- **The align step in the job:** with `align = on`, after a part is picked the camera looks at it and says how far it is from the nozzle (X and Y) and how much it is turned; the place goes to the planned place less that offset and the nozzle turns less that turn. A part that the camera finds further off than `pick_tolerance_mm` is thrown away (it is gone from its feeder, and the next part is fed) and picked again as `pick_retries` and `error_policy` (`alert` or `defer`) say. Off by default: with the step off a job (its plan, its events, its reports and its saved state) is exactly what it was.
- **Settings of the step:** `align` (`off` or `on`), `pick_tolerance_mm` (0.001 to 50), `align_passes` (1 to 5 looks, each one correcting what the last left) and `align_settle_s` (0 to 10 seconds to wait before every look), documented in `examples/machine.cfg`.
- **The time of the step is in the plan:** every look adds `align_settle_s` to the time of the plan (the text report says how much of it is the camera, and the JSON plan has `align_s` only when the step is on) and to the clock of the simulated machine.
- **The simulated camera:** `sim_align_dx_mm`, `sim_align_dy_mm` and `sim_align_deg` (how far every part is from the nozzle), `sim_align_noise_mm` (a random error of each reading, drawn from `sim_seed`) and `sim_camera_fail_rate`; a look can be made to fail by chance or at a chosen look (`Job::camera_mut`). The camera has a generator of its own, so the failures of the picks and the places do not move when it is used.
- **Statistics of the correction:** the parts corrected, the mean and the largest distance, the largest turn, the parts thrown away and the looks without a reading, in the text of `simulate`, in the JSON report (`align`) and in a new section `align` of `--report-csv` that follows the rows that were always there (the header does not change); a saved job keeps them and the corrections of the parts on the nozzles.
- **New error codes:** `PHOENIX-PNP-035` (the camera finds the part further off than the tolerance) and `PHOENIX-PNP-036` (the camera gives no reading of the part), with their meaning in the eight languages and in the eight READMEs.
- **The vision pipeline (`vision`):** measures where a part is under the nozzle and how much it is turned from a grey picture, with the steps of a file: a mask (a rectangle or a circle), a median filter (repeated 1 to 5 times), a threshold (fixed, or the level of Otsu found in each picture; the part may be brighter or darker than the background), the spots of the picture joined along the diagonals too, a range of area for the spot that is the part (a smaller spot is noise) and the smallest rectangle, turned, that holds the spot (found from the convex hull of its pixels). The offset is in millimetres with the first row of the picture on top and the turn positive counter-clockwise; the turn of a rectangle is known up to a half turn (a nearly square part up to a quarter turn), measured from the axis along which its long side lies when it is not turned. Library: `vision::{VisionConfig, Image, render, measure_image, otsu_level, median3, min_area_rect}`.
- **`vision.cfg`, the settings of the pipeline:** `mm_per_px`, `mask` (`none`, `rect x0 y0 x1 y1` or `circle cx cy r`), `filter`, `filter_passes`, `threshold_mode`, `threshold`, `part_is_dark`, `min_area_px`, `max_area_px`, `nominal_long_axis`, `square_ratio` and the picture that the simulator draws (`image_width`, `image_height`, `sim_part_long_mm`, `sim_part_short_mm`, `sim_background`, `sim_foreground`, `sim_noise_levels`). Every key has a strict range, an unknown key or a key written twice is an error, and `examples/vision.cfg` is the file the program writes as its default, kept equal by a test. `simulate --vision FILE` reads it (it is never written over).
- **The simulated camera can measure a picture:** the new setting `vision = off|pipeline` of `machine.cfg` (off by default: no job changes) makes the camera of the align step draw the picture of the part where it is, less what was corrected, with a noise that comes from `sim_seed` and the number of the look, and measure it with the pipeline instead of reading the offset it was given. With the step off nothing changes whatever the setting says, and a saved job resumes with the same readings.
- **Failures of the vision:** a part that is not found, a single spot whose area is out of the range and several spots are looks without a reading that the align step already handles (the part is thrown away and picked again as `pick_retries` and `error_policy` say, and the look is counted among the looks without a reading), each with its own code in the reason of the part put aside and in the log of the machine.
- **`phoenix-pnp vision-test`:** draws the picture of a part from `--size WxH`, `--part LxS` (millimetres), `--mm-per-px`, `--dx`, `--dy`, `--turn`, `--noise` and `--seed` with the settings of `--vision FILE`, measures it and prints what was true, what was measured and the error; it exits with 1 when the pipeline finds no part. New error codes `PHOENIX-PNP-037` (the vision did not find the part), `PHOENIX-PNP-038` (the spot has an area outside the range) and `PHOENIX-PNP-039` (more than one spot could be the part), with their meaning in the eight languages and in the eight READMEs.
- **The work report (`report`):** after a job is simulated, the report says how many parts were placed, put aside, left out by the person, still pending and not to be mounted; where the machine time went (feed and pick, the looks of the camera, the places, the moves of the head, the changes of tip, which add up to the clock of the machine to the microsecond); how many picks worked at the first attempt, after retries or never; the same by package, feeder, nozzle, nozzle tip and board of a panel; the errors that were raised, with their codes `PHOENIX-PNP-NNN` and how many times; the findings before the job; and three histograms (X, Y and turn) of the first reading of the camera for each corrected part. Library: `report::{build, WorkReport, ReportInput, histogram}`.
- **`simulate --report-text FILE` and `--report-html FILE`:** the report as text in the language of `--lang` (the eight languages of the program) and as one page of HTML that holds everything it shows: the style is inside, there is no script, no picture and no link, and every text that comes from a file is escaped. A page is never written over a file that exists (the error is `PHOENIX-PNP-040` and it is told before the job runs); a report is never written over a file it was made from.
- **Sections after the rows of `--report-csv`, and `work` in `--report`:** the header of the CSV does not change and the rows that were always there stay where they were; the rows `work`, `time`, `action`, `error`, `histogram`, `nozzle`, `tip`, `board`, `dnp`, `finding` and `skipped` follow them, and the section of the align step stays the last one. The JSON keeps every key it had and adds `work`.
- **What the job keeps for the report:** the time of each phase, the picks at the first attempt, the first reading of the camera for each part and the errors raised are kept in the saved state too, so that a job resumed anywhere gives the report of the job that never stopped (a state saved before this has none of it and starts it again from zero). A job that nobody touches is the one it always was.
- **Decisions of the person when an error stops the job:** `Job::act` carries out retry, skip the part (it is left out, in the new state `skipped`), put it aside, or switch off the feeder of the step that failed (another feeder of the same kind takes over); an action that does not fit the state of the job is refused. Each decision is counted in the report and the part keeps the reason.
- **The Job tab:** the table shows the state of every placement (waiting, picked, aligned, placed, put aside, left out, failed) and, after the plan, the parts not to be mounted, with the reason as a hint; a bar of progress with the machine time, an estimate of the time left and the parts per hour; how many parts are in each state; and filters by state, by text (reference, value or feeder) and by what is done. The logic is in `gui::jobtab` and is tested without a screen.
- **A dialog for the error that stopped the job:** it names the error with its code in the language of the window, the part, the feeder, the nozzle and the step, and offers the four actions of the person, which are carried out on the simulated job, which then goes on. A button of the tab copies the work report.
- **`--window TAB` and `--steps N` of the picture mode:** the picture can show any tab (`job`, `parts`, `packages`, `feeders`, `panels`, `boards`, `loadsheet`, `machine`, `problems` or `log`) and make up to 100000 steps of the simulated job before it is taken; both need `--shot`.
- **New error codes:** `PHOENIX-PNP-040` (the report file already exists and is not written over) and `PHOENIX-PNP-041` (the action does not fit the state of the job), with their meaning in the eight languages and in the eight READMEs.
- **Boards marked as bad (X-out):** a child of the panel file with `"x_out": true`, or `--x-out A,B/C` (the names are paths: `A`, `P/D`), is left out with everything inside it, the marks of a panel inside it included; the plan, the load sheet and the checks do not see it, and each one is told as `x-out: C is marked as bad and is left out`. Library: `Panel::expand_with(load, x_out)` and `Expanded::x_out`; `Panel::expand` is what it was.
- **Parts that are marks:** a part of a board whose value or package holds a word of `fiducial_words` (default `fiducial`, any case) or whose reference is a prefix of `fiducial_refs` (default `fid, fd`) followed by a number (`FID1`) is a mark of its board and is not placed (`fiducial parts: 4 are marks of their boards and are not placed (A/FID1, ...)`); an empty list turns the rule off, and `examples/machine.cfg` documents the two keys. Library: `marks::{is_fiducial_part, take_fiducial_parts, marks_of}`.
- **Marks of two levels:** the file of `--fiducials` can name the marks of the panel (`f1`) and the marks of a board that are parts (`A/FID1`, `B/C/FID2`): the marks of one board are fitted together (at least two, as the mode says) and move only the parts of that board, the parts of the boards with no measured mark follow the fit of the panel, and each fit is printed (`fiducials of A: 2 marks, similarity fit, ...`). A mark that neither level has is refused, and a board whose marks miss by more than `fiducial_max_residual_mm` is refused by name. Library: `marks::{fit_levels, correct_levels, LevelFit}`.
- **New error codes:** `PHOENIX-PNP-042` (a board named as bad is not in the panel) and `PHOENIX-PNP-043` (every board of the panel is marked as bad), with their meaning in the eight languages and in the eight READMEs.
